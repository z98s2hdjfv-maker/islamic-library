### Section 9: the touchstone and the two senses

Mathnawi 1:299-305. Voice in the text: Rumi, in his own voice. False gold and true are told apart only by a touchstone, which God places in the soul; the sense of this world and the sense of religion; the health of the second comes from the ruin of the body. `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0299` (Mathnawi 1:299); `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0305` (Mathnawi 1:305)

- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): On 299 he recalled the song: 'two forms under the sky, one ash, one spark... two bees, one sting, one honey.' On 300: 'When you are near, one's eyes can see clearly... when you are near one can hear better, the senses are sharper and more receptive... our spiritual senses are sharper when we are near our Creator... we can see better with His eyes, we hear better with His ears, we speak better when we use His voice... The nearness is the key; it answers the rest of section 9.' Rests on: 300, 304; 268.
- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): On the ruin of the body (305): 'The body loves this world, for it has many choices, where the soul only has one... the body has to be silenced in order to hear the one true choice.' And: 'When the nearness is one there are no more choices, which ruins the body... when you are one with the Creator we are back to how we started this journey, from the loins.' Rests on: 305; 298.
- **Claude's analysis** (Claude (the assistant); draft): The reading makes nearness the touchstone. The text supports it: God places the touchstone in the soul (300); the health of that sense is sought 'from the Beloved' (304), and a beloved is stayed near; the two bees are Rumi's own (268); 'go to their source' (298) supports the return. It goes beyond the section in four places. 'We see with His eyes' is the divine saying in al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari no. 6502), not these couplets, though Rumi says it himself later ('by Me he hears and by Me he sees', 1:1938 and 2:1739; who is addressed there was not read); 'His voice' was not found in the wording searched. 'Choices' and 'love of this world' are Housam's reason; the section gives none. 'Silenced' is gentler than Rumi's 'ruin' (305), and 306 adds that the body is built up again. 'One with the Creator' is stronger than the couplets: nearness is affirmed by all the seats, union is language the critics reject, and their words were not searched. 'The loins' is one reading of the covenant (7:172), discussed and limited by Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr and Ibn Taymiyya. Couplets 301-302 were left open here and are covered in section 10. What we hold: the touchstone is nearness to the Creator, by which the spiritual senses are made sharp; the ruin of the body is the silencing of its many choices (Housam's reason, not Rumi's). (Claude's translations.) ### Section 10: the ruin and the rebuilding

Mathnawi 1:306-311. Voice in the text: Rumi, in his own voice. Four pictures of ruin followed by rebuilding (the house and its treasure, the stream, the arrowhead, the fortress), and then Rumi's own remark that God's work has no 'how' (311). `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0306` (Mathnawi 1:306); `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0311` (Mathnawi 1:311)

- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): 'This is the process describing the journey to be with our Creator... silencing the choices... the voices of confusion and deception.' He named the verses he meant: Surat al-Fatiha (the straight path, 1:6-7) and Qur'an 114:4-5, the whisperer who withdraws, who whispers in the breasts of people. Rests on: 309-310. `urn:quran:1:6`; `urn:quran:1:7`; `urn:quran:114:4`; `urn:quran:114:5`
- **Claude's analysis** (Claude (the assistant); draft): In each picture what is taken out is not the body but something lodged in it: the arrowhead (309), the unbeliever holding the fortress (310), the dirt in the stream (308); and the treasure was under the house all along (307), which supports Housam's return to the beginning. So 'voices of confusion and deception' fits the pictures better than 'the body' does, and sharpens section 9: the occupier is silenced, not the fortress. Qur'an 114:4-5 covers the gap left in section 9: the whisper is the speck in the living mouth (301-302), it is 'in the breasts', and the whisperer 'withdraws'. Rumi quotes neither passage in this section; the link is Housam's. Rumi does use the word: 'God called that devil khannas, for he is like the head of that hedgehog' (3:4060; context not read), and waswas or waswasa in about 30 couplets of the Mathnawi. In 311 Rumi sets his own pictures down: God's work has no 'how'. Left open at this point, and answered by Housam in section 11: what is built after the ruin. What we hold: section 10 is the journey to the Creator; what is removed is the voices of confusion and deception; the text agrees that the enemy is the occupier and not the house, and adds that the house is given back, more flourishing. (Claude's translations.) ### Section 11: the bewilderment

Mathnawi 1:312-315. Voice in the text: Rumi, in his own voice. 'The work of religion is nothing but bewilderment': not with the back toward Him, but drowned and drunk with the Friend. Two faces (314); keep watch over faces (315). `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0312` (Mathnawi 1:312); `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0315` (Mathnawi 1:315)

- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): 'When the false choices and noise are silenced and the one true message is now heard clearly, the body and soul are aligned... this is a new feeling of bewilderment and drunk with a friend... a euphoria and harmony that comes when everything is lined perfectly.' (His words ran 'aligned and in constant quarrel'; read as 'no longer in constant quarrel', not confirmed by him.) On 314: 'One is still hearing choices and noise, and the other, the noises and choices are silenced.' On 315: 'The process of trying to reduce the noise.' Rests on: 313; 306-308.
- **Claude's analysis** (Claude (the assistant); draft): A new feeling, not the old confusion: Rumi separates two bewilderments himself (313) and Housam places his after the silencing. 'Euphoria' is Rumi's 'drunk with the Friend'. 'Body and soul aligned' answers what section 10 left open: the house rebuilt (306-307), the water running in the same stream (308). It differs in two things. Harmony against drowning: Rumi's word beside 'drunk' is 'drowned'. Clarity against not knowing: God's work has no 'how' (311) and shows itself now one way, now the contrary (312). Claude's analysis: in 313 the one thing certain is the direction of the face, so the message may be clear as a direction while the work of God stays beyond grasp. 315 names the means, watching faces and service; service is not in Housam's reading. The second half of 314 can be read 'his face is itself the Friend's face' or 'his face is only his own face' (as Claude recalls Nicholson taking it); the library has no commentary to decide. (Claude's translations.) ### Section 12: the devil with Adam's face, and the two wines

Mathnawi 1:316-323. Voice in the text: Rumi, in his own voice. The warning against the false guide: the fowler's call, the stolen words of the dervishes, the woollen lion, Musaylim called Ahmad; the wine of God sealed with musk. 'Its seal is musk' echoes Qur'an 83:26. `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0316` (Mathnawi 1:316); `urn:sufi:rumi.mathnawi:1.b0323` (Mathnawi 1:323)

- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): 316: 'Do not pick any of the choices, as the false choices will become true choice with patience' (read as: the true choice becomes clear with patience; not confirmed by him). 317: 'These false choices are a trap; don't act on them.' 318: 'These false choices are given credence as they are common with the norm.' 319: 'The choices can sound like they have the body's best intentions... a prescription from a self-proclaimed doctor... when taken they create more choices and separate the soul and the body.' 320: 'A true man's nearness filters out all the false choices coming from the base, referred to as the body.' Rests on: 316-319.
- **Reader** (Housam (the owner of this library); draft): Read backwards from the last couplet. 323: 'The Prophet (wine of God), peace be upon him, is pure musk, and it is throughout time, where no patience will change that, for it is the truth, the one message after all the noise is filtered out. Ordinary wine drunk by liars, with patience it is stench and torment, as it makes the body sick and turns into extremities [read as: excrement] to be ejected from the body... The noise takes false faces and tries to snare the bird, but the wine of God will never change and never snare falsely the bird.' 322: 'Musaylim is the ordinary wine drunk... the face that sees other faces... it turns to stench and torment for those that ingest it, or listened to him and followed him... even when he took the Prophet's name falsely... the face looking at other faces... not like the Prophet, peace be upon him... the face who is the face.' Rests on: 322-323; 269.
- **Claude's analysis** (Claude (the assistant); draft): 318 is the sharpest point of the reading: the bird falls for 'the call of its own kind'; the bait is what is familiar, the parrot's own mistake. 319 goes further than the reading: the deceiver uses the dervishes' own words, so words cannot be the touchstone. 'Expelled from the body' joins the two ends of Rumi's commentary: two deer ate the same grass, 'from this one came dung, and from that one pure musk' (269), and 'pure musk' is the same phrase in 323. The word for 'seal' in 323 also means 'end'; Rumi says of Musaylim elsewhere 'look at the end' (4:1696). With 'those who listened to him and followed him' the reading carries the outward warning as well as the inward. It differs in five things. Rumi's plain sense in 316-320 is about people (false guides, a hand given in allegiance); Housam reads them as voices within. 'The base' in 320 are low people known by their work, not the body. Rumi does not say what the wine is; the pairing of 322 and 323 supports taking it as the Prophet, and the step is still the reader's. 'Torment' usually means punishment, and 83:25-26 is about the hereafter. 'The face who is the face' rests on one of the two readings of 314, whose first figure faces the Friend, not other faces; how far the phrase may be taken is a matter on which the seats would differ. (Claude's translations.)
