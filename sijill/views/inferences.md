The seven heavens passed through on the Miʿraj are the same seven the Qur'an names.

**Verdict: holds** (draft). The hadith of the ascent names the lowest heaven and counts to the seventh.

- **ʿAbd al-Qadir al-Jilani** (d. 561 AH): *affirms*. The night of the Miʿraj is 'the night of drawing near', and the prayers were fixed in it. `urn:openiti:0561CabdQadirJilani.Ghunya.Sham19Y0017817-ara1:p03457` (al-Ghunya 2:22)

## 'The heaven full of pathways' (51:7) may mean routes such as wormholes

Hidden routes through creation that lead to other realms.

**Verdict: unsupported** (draft). al-Tabari, from Ibn ʿAbbas: 'of beautiful form'; also 'of ripples', like waves in sand or curled hair.

- **al-Tabari** (d. 310 AH): *qualifies*. 'Of beautiful form'; also 'of ripples (taraʾiq)'. `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p67995` (Tafsir 21:487)

## In the seven heavens he met seven prophets: ʿIsa, Yahya, Yusuf, Idris, Musa, Harun, Ibrahim

One prophet per heaven in that order.

**Verdict: wrong** (draft). Muslim: Adam in the first; ʿIsa and Yahya in the second; Yusuf, Idris, then Harun in the fifth, Musa in the sixth, Ibrahim in the seventh. Eight prophets; Adam is left out and Musa and Harun are swapped.


## 'The sky that returns' (86:11) means the heavens return like the sun

The heavens continually return according to an order woven into creation.

**Verdict: unsupported** (draft). al-Tabari: it returns with the rain clouds and people's provision each year.

## 36:40 points to the earth's rotation

'It is not for the sun' means 'has no need', and 'the night does not overtake the day' excludes a moving sun.

**Verdict: unsupported** (draft). al-Tabari: it is not fitting for the sun to overtake the moon, so that all time would be day.

## 'Seven heavens' appears in seven verses of seven suras

The phrase is not random: it comes seven times, in seven suras.

## Sidrat al-Muntaha is the final boundary of the created universe

Beyond the Sidra belongs to Allah alone; it is the furthest point of creation.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). The texts say it is where what rises from the earth and what descends from above stops. The Kursi, the Throne and al-Firdaws are created and lie beyond it, as the lecture itself says later.

- **ʿAbd Allah ibn Masʿud** (d. 32 AH): *qualifies*. The Sidra is in the sixth heaven: to it ends what ascends from the earth, and to it ends what descends from above. `urn:openiti:0774IbnKathir.TafsirQuran.Shamela0008473-ara1:p04966` (Ibn Kathir 1:734, quoting Muslim)

## The Throne surrounds everything, as the rope hadith shows

A rope lowered from the lowest earth would reach Allah, so the Throne encloses all.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). Built on a weak report that its own transmitters read as 'upon Allah's knowledge'.

- **al-Tirmidhi** (d. 279 AH): *qualifies*. Calls the rope hadith strange, notes al-Hasan did not hear from Abu Hurayra, and reports the reading 'upon Allah's knowledge, power and authority' (as quoted by al-Sakhawi). `urn:openiti:0902Sakhawi.MaqasidHasana.JK001160-ara1:p03407` (Maqasid no. 886)

## 'Day' 365 times, 'days' 30, 'month' 12 show a mathematical miracle

The word counts match the calendar and clearly show a mathematical miracle.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). The counts do come out in the library's text, but only by a chosen rule: it leaves out yawmaʾidh (70 times) and yawmakum, yawmahum (10), and for 'month' the plural and dual (9).

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). Ten of the forty are weak, disputed, strange, fabricated or untraced. The lecture flags only one, saying 25.

**Verdict: holds** (draft). The standard definition, and al-Tirmidhi does call saying 25 strange.


## The 'hand' of Allah is not a body part; it is accepted as Allah intended



**Verdict: holds** (draft). This is the position of the creed works in the library.

**Verdict: holds** (draft). His chains to al-Bukhari and others run 'through Yunus ibn Yahya' in Muhadarat al-abrar (36 records; the attribution of that work is doubted) and in the Futuhat (5 records). The count of 38 hadith was not checked.


## Intercession is true and happens by Allah's permission

**Verdict: mixed** (draft). One of two classical views. The other holds that the wording is from Allah too, and that it differs from the Qur'an in not being recited in prayer or inimitable. The library's method works were not found to settle it.


## The book was finished in Makka in 1202 and holds 101 sayings in three parts



**Verdict: holds** (draft). From general knowledge, not the repo: this matches the standard description of the Mishkat. The book is not in the library.

**Verdict: wrong** (draft). Mishkat is a niche, the word of the Light verse: 'the Niche of Lights'.

**Verdict: wrong** (draft). Muslim, al-Bazzar, Ibn Hibban and al-Bayhaqi all give it from Abu Dharr. Likely a slip between two similar names.

**Verdict: mixed** (draft). Ibn Hibban and al-Bayhaqi give it from Abu Hurayra. Ibn ʿArabi's chain may differ, and his book is not here to check.

**Verdict: mixed** (draft). It follows Ibn ʿArabi's wording in the Futuhat. Abu Nuʿaym's version reads 'you do no deed that wipes out your good deeds more than pride'.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). The phrase does not occur in the library's Cairo text of the Futuhat. It occurs in collections of mixed attribution and in later commentators. The name was given to his teaching by others.


## What does 'I am as My servant thinks of Me' mean?



- **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *affirms*. From saying 26 ('the changing of forms is reported in the hadith of Muslim, in the hadith of intercession') he argues that a divine self-disclosure never repeats. Close to Rumi's reading of saying 13. `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00286` (Futuhat 1:285)
- **Rumi** (d. 672 AH): *affirms*. 'I am as My servant thinks of Me. Each servant has an image and a form of Me; whatever he imagines Me to be, I am there... O My servants, purify your imaginings, for they are My dwelling.' Allah meets each servant in the form of his belief. (Claude's translation from the Persian.) `urn:sufi:rumi.fihi:d10.p005` (Fihi ma fihi)

## Is the ether an Islamic teaching?



- **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *rejects*. 'The sphere of the ether, whose spirit is heat and dryness; it is the sphere of fire.' For him the ether is only the sphere of fire, not a universal substance. `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00122` (Futuhat 1:121)
- **Said Nursi** (d. 1379 AH): *affirms*. As the lecture reports him: the water under the Throne is the ether, a universal substance. His works (Isharat al-iʿjaz, the Thirtieth Flash) are not in the library, so this is untested. 

## What was the first created thing?



- **ʿAbd Allah ibn ʿAbbas** (d. 68 AH): *affirms*. Water. Through al-Suddi from Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn Masʿud and other Companions: 'His Throne was on the water, and He had created nothing before the water. When He willed to create, He brought smoke out of the water and it rose above it, so He called it heaven; then He dried the water and made it one earth.' `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p01574` (Tafsir 1:461)
- **al-Tirmidhi** (d. 279 AH): *affirms*. The Pen, as the hadith of ʿUbada reports: 'the first thing Allah created was the Pen'. Entered under al-Tirmidhi as a compiler who transmits and grades it (fair and strange), with Abu Dawud. `urn:hadith:0279Tirmidhi.Sunan:p08820`; `urn:hadith:0275AbuDawudSijistani.Sunan:4700`
- **ʿAbd al-Qadir al-Jilani** (d. 561 AH): *qualifies*. Only Sirr al-asrar speaks to this, and its attribution to him is doubtful: 'the first thing Allah created was my spirit, my light, the Pen, the Intellect; what is meant by them all is one thing, the Muhammadan reality'. It quotes these as hadith with no chain. `urn:shamela:jilani.sirrasrar:r00009`
- **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *qualifies*. The universal substance is the dust (habaʾ), his name for the philosophers' prime matter, in which the Muhammadan reality is the first to receive the light. There is a first substance, but it is not the ether. `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00119`; `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00120`

## Is there an unseen realm behind the visible one?



- **al-Ghazali** (d. 505 AH): *affirms*. 'The eye is two eyes, outer and inner. - **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *affirms*. The water is the root of life and of the elements: 'His Throne was on the water... so the Throne and all it holds came alive'. `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00134`; `urn:shamela:ibnarabi.futuhat.arabiyya:r00981`
- **Rumi** (d. 672 AH): *affirms*. 'The world of creation has sides and directions; know the world of the Command and the attributes to be without direction.' 'His form is on the earth and his soul in the placeless, a placelessness beyond the fancy of the wayfarers.' He affirms the unseen realm in his own Persian terms (ʿalam-i amr, la-makan: 'placeless' occurs 92 times across his works) and denies it any location.
