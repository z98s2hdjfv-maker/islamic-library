## Everyone has the fitra completely and equally



**Verdict: overstated** (draft). All have the fitra, but its strength differs (Rumi, al-Ghazali).

- **al-Ghazali** (d. 505 AH): *qualifies*. Some are born with perfect character and need no teaching. Ihyaʾ, p. 79 (record id pending)
- **Rumi** (d. 672 AH): *qualifies*. Souls who heard Alast forget it wholly, partly or not at all, 'according to their rank and essence'. Fihi ma fihi, ch. 14 (record id pending)

## Fitra, hanif and Islam are one thing

The fitra of 30:30 is Islam itself; ḥanīfan and fiṭrata llāh name the same thing.

**Verdict: holds** (draft). Backed by the Companions and Followers; the grammar argument is weaker than the inference.

- **Muʿadh ibn Jabal** (d. 18 AH): *affirms*. 'Sincerity (ikhlas) is the fitra', quoting 30:30; ʿUmar answered 'you spoke truly'. `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p56462` (al-Tabari 18:493); `urn:openiti:0751IbnQayyimJawziyya.ShifaCalil.JK000100-ara1:p00516` (Shifaʾ al-ʿalil 1:287)
- **ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab** (d. 23 AH): *affirms*. Approved Muʿadh's answer that sincerity is the fitra. `urn:openiti:0751IbnQayyimJawziyya.ShifaCalil.JK000100-ara1:p00516` (Shifaʾ al-ʿalil 1:287)
- **Abu Hurayra** (d. 58 AH): *affirms*. Recites 30:30 immediately after 'every child is born on the fitra'. `urn:hadith:0256Bukhari.Sahih:1292` (al-Bukhari, Fath al-Bari no. 1358)
- **ʿIkrima** (d. 105 AH): *affirms*. The fitra of God is the religion of God. `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p56472` (al-Tabari 18:495)
- **al-Alusi** (d. 1270 AH): *qualifies*. Badal (ḥanīfan = fiṭrata llāh) is only a permissible parsing; the more natural reading is ighrāʾ, 'hold fast to the fitra'. The identity rests on the Companions, not the syntax. Ruh al-maʿani 21:38

## Inborn knowledge of God is more precise than the greatest theologian's

Nothing correct a theologian could say cannot be dug out of oneself.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). The leaning toward the Creator is agreed (even Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr calls it the consensus of Ahl al-Sunna); inborn knowledge of the details is rejected by the Qur'an, Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr and Ibn Taymiyya; only Ibn ʿArabi goes as far as the lecturer.

- **The Qur'an** (d. 0 AH): *rejects*. 'God brought you out of your mothers' wombs knowing nothing' (16:78); 'you did not know what the Book was, nor faith' (42:52). `urn:quran:16:78`; `urn:quran:42:52`
- **Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr** (d. 463 AH): *rejects*. That a child is born knowing God 'is what observation and reason belie'; the most correct reading is soundness and uprightness. Yet he grants, as the consensus of Ahl al-Sunna, an inborn pull toward a Creator once reason matures. `urn:openiti:0463IbnCabdBarr.TamhidMuwatta.JK000585-ara1:p01516` (al-Tamhid 18:88 (the chapter runs 18:57-97; 'soundness and uprightness' is on 18:70))
- **al-Ghazali** (d. 505 AH): *qualifies*. Faith lies hidden in the soul as a nearby readiness, recovered by remembering; but no one draws near to God by instinct alone, only through acquired knowledge. Ihyaʾ, pp. 168, 497 (study's page refs; record ids pending)
- **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *affirms*. An infant's faith is 'more complete than an adult's, without doubt'; the spirit is created knowing and believing in God's oneness. al-Futuhat, Shamela ed. p. 670 (record id pending)
- **Ibn Taymiyya** (d. 728 AH): *qualifies*. The fitra contains acknowledging and loving the Creator, as a newborn desires the milk that suits it; but it is not knowledge of the religion at birth; it develops, and messengers complete it. `urn:openiti:0728IbnTaymiyya.DarTacarud.JK000362-ara1:p09798` (Darʾ taʿarud 8:384)

## Lā tabdīl: the fitra can be damaged but never replaced

So it is always recoverable; tabdīl (replacement) is excluded, taghyīr (change) is not.

**Verdict: mixed** (draft). Holds as Ibn al-Qayyim's reading; al-Tabari, Mujahid and others read the phrase as a prohibition, so the distinction is a choice between readings, not a given.

- **Mujahid ibn Jabr** (d. 104 AH): *rejects*. 'No changing God's creation' means God's religion: a command not to change it, not a statement that it cannot be replaced. `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p56470` (al-Tabari 18:496)
- **al-Tabari** (d. 310 AH): *rejects*. Reads lā tabdīl as no taghyīr of God's religion, a prohibition; he does not distinguish tabdīl from taghyīr. `urn:openiti:0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0007798-ara1:p56470` (al-Tabari 18:496 (Mujahid's report, which al-Tabari follows))
- **Ibn al-Qayyim** (d. 751 AH): *affirms*. What people are born upon is God's creation, which no one but He could replace, and He does not; unlike exchanging unbelief for faith, which happens often. `urn:openiti:0751IbnQayyimJawziyya.ShifaCalil.JK000100-ara1:p00527` (Shifaʾ al-ʿalil 1:295); `urn:openiti:0751IbnQayyimJawziyya.ShifaCalil.JK000100-ara1:p00448` (Shifaʾ al-ʿalil 1:253-254)

## All misguidance comes from society, not from human nature

Parents and society corrupt; the self is not a source of going astray.

**Verdict: overstated** (draft). The Qur'an and Ibn ʿArabi place a second source within the self; the lecturer's own remedy (purifying the heart) assumes it.

- **The Qur'an** (d. 0 AH): *rejects*. 'He inspired it with its wickedness and its piety' (91:8); 'the self commands evil' (12:53): a source of going astray within. `urn:quran:91:8`; `urn:quran:12:53`
- **Ibn ʿArabi** (d. 638 AH): *rejects*. 'Man's disobedience comes from what he was created upon; his obedience from what he was created for.' al-Futuhat, Shamela ed. p. 74 (record id pending)
